Introduction

Everyday conditions, mapped by students.

NUS Study Space Map helps students identify suitable study spaces using direct experience: seat availability, noise level, access to power sockets, suitability for different study activities, and overall quality.

01 / Project Objective

Complement formal directories with lived experience.

Conditions such as crowding and noise vary across locations and over time, making them difficult to capture through official campus directories. User-generated observations create a shared source of practical information for students choosing where to study.

The project also demonstrates how participatory data collection can complement formal campus information by capturing everyday experiences that are highly relevant to users.

02 / Community Partners

A student-led initiative with campus relevance.

Main participants

NUS students who use study spaces across the campus.

Project management

A student-led initiative maintaining the survey structure, reviewing submissions, and presenting information.

Potential partners

NUS Libraries and relevant campus facilities or student-service units.

Main audience

The wider NUS student community seeking study spaces that match their preferences.

03 / Data Gathering and Management Plan

Collect, review, and display practical observations.

1

Collect

Participants identify a study space and report its name, description, seat availability, noise level, power socket availability, suitable activities, and overall rating.

2

Review

During a possible four-week pilot, submissions would be reviewed for incomplete, duplicate, or clearly inappropriate entries.

3

Display

Aggregated results would be displayed through the Ushahidi map and data interface.

04 / Prototype Application

A live Ushahidi deployment for map-based reporting.

The prototype uses Ushahidi for web-based submissions and direct location marking. Submitted reports are stored within the deployment and viewed spatially through the map interface.

05 / Participant Manual

Submit an observation in four steps.

Step 01

Start a New Submission

Click “Add new post” on the left-hand menu to begin a new submission.

Step 02

Select the Survey

Select “NUS Study Space Map” to open the study space reporting form.

Step 03

Enter Study Space Information

Enter the name and a short description. Mark the location and complete the required questions on seat availability, noise level, power sockets, overall rating, suitable study activities, and optional comments.

Step 04

Submit the Report

Review the information and click “Submit” to add the report to the platform.

06 / Visualization of Submissions

Study-space reports viewed spatially.

The prototype map interface displays submitted reports by location across the NUS campus.
